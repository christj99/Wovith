# Wovith Positioning and Go-to-Market
### Competitive landscape, audience priority, messaging hierarchy, launch sequence

---

## 0. About this document

This document specifies how Wovith enters the market. It covers the competitive landscape as it stands in mid-2026, where Wovith sits in it, who Wovith is for in priority order, the pricing model, the launch sequence, the distribution channels, and the messaging hierarchy that translates the conceptual positioning into actual launch copy.

The document is opinionated and tactical. It picks sides on questions where ambiguity would compound (which audience first, which channels, what to call the product to a stranger). It also names anti-goals — the audiences Wovith is *not* trying to capture at launch and the comparisons it's *not* trying to win.

The strategic premise: the personal AI assistant market is real and growing (projected $4.84B in 2026, $19.63B by 2030 per Research and Markets; 41.9% CAGR per analyst consensus), but it's currently dominated by chat-shaped products that surface answers in conversation threads or email-shaped products that automate inbox triage. The spatial-canvas-as-lens-runtime form factor is open — no incumbent owns it, and the cognitive shape of "see your stuff, the way you want to see it" is fundamentally different from the cognitive shape of "ask the bot, get an answer." That gap is Wovith's market.

## 1. The market in one paragraph

In May 2026, a knowledge worker with $30/month and an hour to set things up has roughly fifteen "personal AI" products competing for their seat. Most are either chat (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity) or autonomous email agents (Lindy, Alfred, Personal AI Assistant by Arahi). Some are calendar optimizers (Reclaim, Motion), some are voice-first (Martin), some are PKM-with-AI (Tana, Mem, Saner), some are knowledge worker productivity suites (Claude Cowork, Notion AI). The shared assumption across the entire category is that personal AI is something you *talk to* — through chat, voice, or watch-it-work-the-inbox automation. None of these products is shaped as a spatial canvas where the user wears configurable lenses over their data and the AI is plumbing inside those lenses, not a conversational counterpart. The market reads personal AI as conversation; Wovith reads it as software you author. That difference is the basis of Wovith's positioning.

## 2. The competitive landscape

Three concentric rings.

### 2.1 Inner ring: direct competitors

Products that compete for the same user need (personal AI that reads your stuff and helps you act on it) and could be substituted for Wovith.

- **Vellum** — open source personal AI with persistent memory, its own identity, multi-channel. Closest in ambition. Different form factor (chat-shaped, reaches out via Telegram/Slack rather than living on a canvas).
- **Lindy** — autonomous email agent with cross-app actions, 1,500+ integrations. Different focus (email-first, productivity automation; not malleable software).
- **Alfred** — autonomous email work specifically, $24.99/mo. Direct competition for inbox-focused use cases but narrow scope.
- **Personal AI Assistant (by Arahi)** — proactive multi-app personal AI, scored highest on "Memory + Agency" rubrics. Closest commercial competitor by ambition.
- **Claude Cowork** — local file management agent, 1M context, computer use. Different (file-system focus, not canvas; uses Anthropic's UI).

### 2.2 Middle ring: adjacent products

Products that don't compete directly but solve neighboring problems and could pull users away if those neighboring problems matter more than Wovith's.

- **Tana** — supertag-based PKM, deepest existing "malleable PKM" product. Pulls Wovith's most likely early adopters but is outline-shaped, not canvas-shaped. Onboarding cliff is the well-documented weakness.
- **Notion + Notion AI** — the workhorse of personal knowledge work. Different cognitive shape (page/database) but absorbs use cases Wovith could serve.
- **tldraw computer** — spatial cells + AI, the structurally closest cousin. Content-workflow shaped, not lens-shaped — but worth watching as a potential converging form factor.
- **Obsidian / Roam / Logseq** — note-graph products with active malleable-software communities. Wovith's earliest audience lives here.
- **Glamorous Toolkit** — moldable development environment. Ideologically aligned ancestor; not a direct competitor but the philosophical lineage Wovith claims.
- **Raycast** — productivity launcher with AI commands and 1000+ extensions. Different surface (command palette, not canvas) but overlaps in "AI-augmented personal computing for power users."

### 2.3 Outer ring: substitutes

Products users might use *instead* of any personal AI tool because their existing workflow is good enough.

- Plain Gmail and Calendar with native AI features (Gemini-in-Gmail, Google's "Help me write")
- Apple's revamped Siri (post-iOS 26.4) with cross-app context awareness
- ChatGPT with the user's manual context-switching
- Just spreadsheets and a calendar

These are not features in the head-to-head sense, but they're the ambient alternative — what users do if they don't adopt any of the products in the inner or middle rings.

### 2.4 The competitive map visualized

If we plot two axes — *conversational ←→ spatial* and *passive ←→ proactive* — the products distribute roughly as:

- **Conversational + passive:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini
- **Conversational + proactive:** Vellum, Lindy, Alfred, Personal AI Assistant
- **Spatial + passive:** Notion, Tana, Obsidian, Roam, Logseq, tldraw computer
- **Spatial + proactive:** *open quadrant — this is where Wovith sits*

The "spatial + proactive" quadrant is genuinely empty in May 2026. Tana is spatial but mostly passive (the user composes; the system doesn't propose). tldraw computer is spatial and has some agentic capabilities but is workflow-shaped. None of the proactive personal AIs are spatial — they all surface through chat, email, or push notification.

Wovith's competitive moat is this quadrant. Owning it credibly requires shipping a product where (a) the spatial canvas is the primary surface, (b) the agentic behavior is real and trusted (not just demo magic), and (c) the malleable-software premise is fluent and easily authored, not an academic abstraction.

## 3. Wovith's positioning statement

The positioning statement, in canonical form (as used by every messaging surface):

> **For people who want to see their digital life their own way, Wovith is a personal canvas where you wear configurable "lenses" over your stuff — your emails, files, calendar, anything you connect — and the lenses adapt to you. Unlike chat AI that answers questions or task automation that takes actions, Wovith lets you author the views you live in.**

The components decoded:

- **"For people who want to see their digital life their own way"** — the audience self-selects. Casual users who want defaults won't read past this; the target users will.
- **"Personal canvas where you wear configurable 'lenses'"** — establishes the form factor concretely.
- **"Over your stuff — your emails, files, calendar, anything you connect"** — names the connectors. "Stuff" is deliberate (voice-doc compliant).
- **"The lenses adapt to you"** — implies the agentic, the calibration, without overpromising autonomy.
- **"Unlike chat AI...or task automation"** — explicitly differentiates from the two dominant category shapes.
- **"Lets you author the views you live in"** — frames authoring as living, not configuring.

The positioning is read as a single thought, not as a set of features. Every shorter version (tagline, social post, App Store description) derives from this paragraph.

## 4. Tagline candidates

Shorter forms for different surfaces. Listed in order of commitment level.

**Strong commitment:**

> *See your stuff, your way.*

Reads as a benefit, not a feature. Three short words. Echoes the positioning paragraph's heart. Suitable for the homepage hero, the App Store name field, and any surface where one line has to carry the whole product.

**Medium commitment:**

> *A canvas for your digital life.*

More concrete about form factor. Suitable for the App Store subtitle, the X/Twitter bio, the press blurb.

> *Wear lenses over your stuff.*

For audiences that already know what lenses are (returning users, mid-funnel marketing). Not for cold first-impressions.

**Weak commitment / for specific contexts:**

> *Personal computing, your way.*

For developer-oriented surfaces. Echoes the Ink & Switch lineage.

> *Calm, agentic, yours.*

Three-adjective shorthand. Useful for footer text, hashtags, internal reference.

The decision: **"See your stuff, your way"** is the primary tagline. Other variants are surface-specific.

## 5. Target audiences in priority order

The launch strategy depends on hitting one audience hard before broadening. Five audiences, ranked by likelihood of activating-and-staying within 30 days.

### 5.1 Audience 1: Malleable software natives

**Who:** Active users of Tana, Roam, Logseq, Obsidian, Notion power-user community, Pharo/Smalltalk enthusiasts, Ink & Switch readers, the kind of person who has written a Glamorous Toolkit blog post or contributed to an Obsidian plugin.

**Why first:** They will get it. The malleable software premise is already their worldview. They've tried the alternatives and found them inadequate. They're the most likely to push through the inevitable rough edges of a v1 product because they're philosophically aligned with the project.

**Size:** Small. Maybe 50,000-100,000 people globally who actively participate in these communities.

**How to reach them:** Discord and Slack communities (Tana, Roam, Obsidian, Pharo), Hacker News, the Ink & Switch newsletter readership, malleable-software-adjacent Twitter/X (Geoffrey Litt, Matthew Wong, Maggie Appleton, etc.), specific subreddits (/r/PKMS, /r/ObsidianMD).

**Pitch language:** Lean into the lineage. The Ink & Switch citation is doing real work here. The Glamorous Toolkit reference signals taste. The phrase "personal lens runtime" reads as evocative, not abstract.

### 5.2 Audience 2: Disappointed personal-AI shoppers

**Who:** People who have tried Lindy, Alfred, Vellum, or Personal AI Assistant, and felt the product was "fine but" — fine but feels like a chat thread bolted on top of email; fine but doesn't surface what I care about; fine but the agent's autonomy doesn't match my comfort level.

**Why second:** These users have already made the cognitive leap that "I want personal AI" and have done the work of connecting accounts and granting scopes. They have an opinion about what's missing. They will compare Wovith favorably against products they've already paid for and quit.

**Size:** Larger than Audience 1, maybe 500,000-1M globally based on the personal AI category's total user base.

**How to reach them:** Review/comparison content (Arahi's comparison articles, Vellum's blog, alfred's blog, the Personal AI Assistant reviews), AI Twitter, X posts that compare personal AI tools, content on Substack about productivity setups.

**Pitch language:** Emphasize the difference. "If you've tried personal AI and felt the chat-thread metaphor wasn't right, Wovith is what comes after." Concrete contrast with named alternatives is acceptable here — Audience 1 doesn't need the comparison; Audience 2 wants it.

### 5.3 Audience 3: AI-curious knowledge workers with overflowing inboxes

**Who:** People who have not yet adopted a personal AI product but feel the cognitive overload of email, calendar, scattered files. They might be ICs at tech companies, freelance consultants, founders, designers, researchers, journalists, writers, doctors, lawyers.

**Why third:** Larger audience, lower self-selection. They aren't already malleable-software native, so the conceptual jump is bigger. The onboarding has to do most of the work. But there are many of them, and they pay.

**Size:** Tens of millions globally.

**How to reach them:** Product Hunt at launch, App Store optimization, content marketing on workflow blogs, founder-led content on X / LinkedIn, podcast appearances (Lex Fridman, Acquired, Cortex, etc. depending on relationship), targeted Google Ads (later).

**Pitch language:** Defer the malleable-software philosophy. Lead with concrete pain points: "your inbox is a graveyard of dropped threads, your calendar tells you what's happening but not what's worth attention." Then offer Wovith as the surface that fixes those problems. The lens vocabulary is introduced later, after the user has felt the value.

### 5.4 Audience 4: Developers who want to author lenses

**Who:** Software engineers, indie hackers, developer-adjacent technical writers, people who would use Raycast extensions or write Obsidian plugins or contribute to homebrew formulas. The kind of person who will share lenses they've authored.

**Why fourth:** Strategically important for the lens garden's eventual supply side, but not the bulk of revenue. Reaching them requires showing the authoring story (DSL, NL bridge, sharing), which is most of the v2 polish work. Better as a v2/v3 audience than a v1 priority — by the time the garden ships in v3+, this audience will already have been authoring and exporting lenses privately for a year, building the seed material that makes the garden launch credible.

**How to reach them:** Hacker News (always, but especially when the lens garden ships at v3+), specific developer communities (Astro, Solid, Svelte, Rust subreddits depending on what Wovith is built on), GitHub stars, conference talks.

### 5.5 Audience 5: Mainstream consumers

**Who:** Most people. Users who don't read Hacker News, don't know what malleable software is, don't have a "stack."

**Why last:** Conceptually the hardest sell. Requires the most onboarding work to land. The unit economics also matter — these users churn faster if the product doesn't immediately deliver. v3+ priority. Not a launch concern.

## 6. The pricing model

The pricing model needs to support the product's ambition while not gating early adopters out.

### 6.1 Commitment

**Wovith Free**

- One canvas
- Up to three connected services (at v1 this is effectively "all of them" — Drive, Gmail, Calendar — since the cap matches the available v1 connector set; the cap becomes meaningful at v2 when additional connectors ship)
- Up to five lenses
- Up to 30 cells total
- Single-device (multi-device sync requires Pro; cross-device transfer via `.wovith-backup` export still works)
- Standard agentic budget (50 units/day soft cap, 100 units/day hard cap — a "unit" approximates one Haiku-class agent call; Sonnet calls cost ~5 units and Opus ~15)
- Local-first storage and full feature access to authored lenses
- Starter lens pack access (install any of the 30+ curated lenses that ship with Wovith)

**Wovith Pro: $24/month or $216/year (25% off)**

- Unlimited canvases, connectors, lenses, cells
- Higher agentic budget (500 units/day soft cap, 1,000 units/day hard cap)
- Background mining and re-mining
- Sync across devices via the cloud relay
- Private lens export and sharing (.wovith-lens files)
- Lens-as-prompt-export
- Priority connector support

**Wovith Trust (future, post-v2): $48/month**

- Everything in Pro
- Highest agentic budget (5,000 units/day hard cap, no soft-cap degradation — Trust users never see Wovith silently downgrade their cells)
- Custom MCP server hosting
- Lens collaboration with named co-editors
- Family plan equivalents (3-5 seats)

### 6.2 Why these numbers

**$24/month** is the median for "useful personal AI assistant" in 2026. Lindy starts at $20, Alfred is $24.99, Personal AI Assistant is in this range. Tana is $14.99/mo but doesn't include agentic computation. ChatGPT Plus is $20/mo and is the implicit anchor. Wovith pricing it at $24/mo signals "premium but not luxury" — the audiences targeted will not balk, and lighter users have a free tier that's genuinely useful.

**The free tier matters.** Personal AI products that gate aggressively at the entry point have struggled (Vellum has open source as compensation; Lindy gates more aggressively and has worse word-of-mouth). The free tier is calibrated to be genuinely usable for someone who wants Wovith as a secondary lens-runtime alongside their main work setup, while leaving headroom for an upgrade decision when they want more.

**The yearly discount** is conventional but worth keeping. 25% off is generous enough to convert serious users without erasing the margin on price-sensitive ones.

**Wovith Trust at $48/mo** is a future commitment. It exists to capture power users who are pushing the agentic budget and want collaboration. v2 priority, not v1.

### 6.3 What the pricing model is not

- **Not freemium-with-a-cliff.** The free tier doesn't downgrade or expire. Users who never upgrade are welcome.
- **Not per-seat enterprise.** Wovith is personal at launch. Enterprise/team plans are explicitly v3+ territory.
- **Not usage-based.** The agentic budget is a soft cap with a clear warning before throttling, not a meter that bills.
- **Not data-monetized.** Wovith never sells or shares user data, doesn't run ads, doesn't have a "free with telemetry" model.

The commitment: the user pays for the software. The software does not pay itself by extracting value from the user's data.

## 8. The launch sequence

The actual play-by-play.

### 8.1 Pre-launch (now through v1 ship)

- **Build in public** on X/Twitter. Chris posts about specific Wovith design decisions, the inverse lens mining algorithm, the DSL, the security model. The audience is malleable-software-curious developers; they self-select to the waitlist.
- **Waitlist on wovith.app** with a one-paragraph description, an email field, and a Twitter handle field. No marketing emails until launch.
- **Targeted preview access** for Audience 1: invite 50-100 people from the Ink & Switch / Tana / Obsidian communities for a private preview. These users help seed the starter pack with lenses they've authored and provide the most valuable feedback.
- **Document everything publicly** — the design docs, the DSL spec, the security model. Treat documentation as marketing. Audience 1 reads docs.

### 8.2 v1 launch (target: roughly two quarters out from now)

**Day -7 to Day 0 (the launch week):**

- Day -7: Pre-launch teaser. The waitlist gets a one-paragraph note: *"Launching Wovith next week. If you're on this list, you're in the first cohort."*
- Day -3: Founder writes a long-form essay for Stratechery / Substack / personal blog: *"Why I built a canvas instead of a chatbot"* — articulates the positioning thesis.
- Day -1: The waitlist gets the activation links. Audience 1 starts onboarding.
- Day 0: Public launch. Show HN post. Product Hunt launch. X/Twitter announcement thread. Wovith.app goes from waitlist to sign-up.

**Day 0 to Day 30:**

- Triage feedback rapidly. The first cohort will find bugs, voice copy that doesn't land, mining proposals that miss. Fix in real time. Be visible in the response.
- Highlight starter pack lenses. Wovith publishes a "Lens of the Week" on X and on the wovith.app homepage, drawn from the starter pack.
- Founder office hours: 30 min/week on X Spaces or Discord, answering questions in real time. Builds connection with the early audience.

**Day 30 to Day 90:**

- Move to Audience 2. Comparison content gets published (with deliberate restraint — no trash-talking competitors). Wovith starts appearing in comparison lists.
- Press outreach. Stratechery, Verge, Tech Review. The Ink & Switch angle gets headlined.
- iOS launch. Audience 3 needs both stores.

**Day 90+:**

- Audience 3 push. Paid acquisition pilot. ASO investment. Founder-led podcasts.
- v2 development against community-driven priorities (co-lenses, the export-as-prompt feature polish, the collaboration story; the full lens garden is a v3+ workstream).

### 8.3 Launch metrics that matter

Not vanity metrics. The specific things being tracked:

- **Waitlist → sign-up conversion** (target: 40%+ on launch day)
- **Sign-up → first-lens-accepted** (target: 85%; from the onboarding doc)
- **Day 7 retention** (target: 60%; from the onboarding doc)
- **Day 30 retention** (target: 35%)
- **Free → Pro conversion** (target: 8% within first 60 days for active users)
- **Lens-author rate by Day 14** (target: 30%; from the onboarding doc)
- **Lens publication rate by Day 30** (target: 5% of Pro users publish at least one lens)
- **Word-of-mouth** (qualitative — are Audience 1 users telling Audience 2 users?)

### 8.4 Launch anti-goals

Things explicitly *not* being optimized for at launch:

- **App Store rankings.** The store presence matters as a credibility signal, not as a primary acquisition channel. Wovith does not pursue "top 10 productivity app" rankings.
- **Sub-$5 unit economics.** The product is priced at premium personal-AI median. No race to the bottom.
- **Enterprise procurement.** Wovith doesn't have a sales team at launch. Inbound enterprise interest is routed to a "we'll get to enterprise later" form.
- **Comparing favorably on Lindy's metrics.** Lindy is great at autonomous email work. Wovith does not try to be better at that specific thing. Wovith is shaped differently.
- **Generic AI brand association.** Wovith is not "the AI app" generically. The shape (canvas) is the point.

The messaging cascades from the positioning statement (section 3) through three tiers.

> *See your stuff, your way.*
>
> *Wovith is a personal canvas where you wear configurable lenses over your emails, files, and calendar. The lenses adapt to you.*
>
> **[ Join the waitlist ]**

Short. Concrete. The visual underneath shows an actual canvas with actual lenses on it — not a stock illustration, not a vague gradient. The user sees the product before they read the second line.

### 9.2 The "what is it" paragraph

For surfaces that have a paragraph (App Store description, Twitter bio expansion, press kit summary):

> *Wovith is a personal lens runtime. Instead of asking AI a question and waiting for an answer, you build "lenses" — configurable views over your stuff that adapt to your situation. A morning lens shows what needs attention today. A work lens cuts the noise to what matters in your day. A reading lens collects everything you said you'd get to. Each lens is yours to author or fork from other people's. Wovith makes the lens, runs the cells inside it, and stays out of your way.*

This is the first paragraph someone reads who's curious enough to look past the tagline. It does three jobs: establishes the form factor, gives three concrete examples, hints at the social layer.

### 9.3 The "why now" paragraph

For surfaces that need the strategic context (press, longer-form content, investor decks):

> *Personal AI in 2026 is shaped like a conversation. You ask, it answers. Or it works the inbox while you sleep. These are useful, and incomplete. What's missing is a surface where the user is the architect — where you compose the views you live in, and the AI is the plumbing inside those views, not a counterpart sitting across the table. Wovith is built around that premise. The lens is the unit. The user is the author. The AI is everywhere and nowhere — running cells, drafting suggestions, surfacing what's worth noticing — but it's never the thing you're talking to.*

### 9.4 The "for whom" paragraph

For surfaces that need to qualify the audience:

> *Wovith is for people who feel the limits of chat AI. Who have a lot going on and want to see it their way, not the way a default productivity tool decides. Who are curious enough to author their first lens within a week, and patient enough to let Wovith propose the lens before they author. If "I want to see my digital life through a lens of my own making" sounds like a sentence you'd say, Wovith is for you.*

## 11. The starter lens pack (v1) and the lens garden (v3+)

A specific scoping note that aligns this document with the concept doc's build priorities.

The concept doc places the full lens **marketplace** at v3+, with the rationale that genuine network effects require a critical user base and a meaningful submission/curation pipeline. That call stands. What launches with v1 is **not** the marketplace. What launches with v1 is a **starter lens pack** — a smaller, simpler, fully-curated artifact that serves a distinct function.

### 11.1 The v1 starter lens pack

The starter pack is a curated set of 30-50 lenses, authored by the Wovith team and the Audience 1 preview cohort, that ships *as part of the product*. New users can install lenses from this pack with a single tap from the lens overview's "Browse starter lenses" affordance.

Coverage:
- Common use cases (morning brief, decisions today, reading queue, client prep, project status)
- A few range-signaling lenses (commit reading, news-with-take, dropped threads)
- Connector-specific demonstrations (one lens that showcases each of Drive, Gmail, Calendar individually)

Critically, the starter pack is *not* user-publishable. Users cannot submit lenses to it. It is the Wovith team's curated library, refreshed quarterly. This is the "demo content" that ships with the product — analogous to the templates shipped with Notion or the starter projects shipped with Figma, not analogous to an app store.

The starter pack delivers most of the *day-one product value* that a full marketplace would deliver (immediate access to useful lenses authored by people who get it) without requiring any of the moderation, publishing, ratings, search, or anti-abuse infrastructure a real marketplace needs.

### 11.2 The lens garden (v3+)

The full lens garden — user-publishing, forking, ratings, discovery, community moderation, supply-chain security review — is v3+. The security doc's section 9 ("The marketplace supply-chain") is forward-looking and describes the garden's architecture *when* it ships.

The transition from starter pack to garden is gradual. v2 may add a "user-submitted lenses" pipeline with manual review (small scale, manually curated). v3 opens this up to the full pattern described in the security doc.

### 11.3 What this means for launch marketing

The launch copy should *not* talk about the lens garden, the marketplace, or community-published lenses. The story is:

- *"Wovith ships with 30+ ready-made lenses you can install in one tap. Author your own from there."*

Not:

- *"Join the lens garden — share your lenses, fork others' lenses, build a community."*

The community story is genuinely valuable but is not the v1 story. Promising it at v1 sets expectations the product doesn't yet deliver. Underpromise here, overdeliver later.

### 11.4 The "Lens of the Week" content cadence

A weekly piece of content highlighting one lens — its design, the problem it solves, how to install it — runs across the Wovith blog and X. At v1, the highlighted lens always comes from the starter pack. At v2, community-submitted lenses can be featured. This content cadence does not depend on the garden being live; it works either way.

### 11.5 Lens authoring as the social affordance at v1

What v1 *does* expose: the ability to author lenses and share them privately by export. A user who builds a useful lens can export it as a `.wovith-lens` file and share it with another user (email, DM, however). The recipient imports the file and runs the lens locally. This is genuine lens-sharing without requiring the marketplace infrastructure.

This is consistent with the lens-as-prompt-export feature in the security doc (section 7) — same underlying mechanism, deployed for human-to-human lens sharing as well as for AI-tool-to-AI-tool context portability.

A lens shared this way:
- Carries the cell expressions and the connector requirements
- Does *not* carry data snapshots unless the exporter explicitly includes them
- Requires the importer to re-authorize the connectors against their own accounts
- Is fully sandboxed by the DSL (no arbitrary code, no network calls outside MCP)

This v1 sharing model is the precursor to the full lens garden — it tests whether users actually want to share lenses, in a way that doesn't yet require building the marketplace.

## 12. The competitive response

What happens when Lindy or Personal AI Assistant launches a spatial canvas feature in response to Wovith?

### 12.1 The likely response

Vellum or Lindy add a "dashboard" or "workspace" view. They will. The chat is hard to give up — it's the surface they've optimized for, and their users are accustomed to it — so the response is most likely a *secondary* spatial view added to the existing chat surface. The canvas as a *primary* form factor requires a different product orientation than they've built.

### 12.2 Wovith's defense

The defense isn't a feature. It's the cumulative design coherence — the lens vocabulary, the freshness palette, the inverse mining, the closed-at-the-bottom DSL, the local-first architecture, and (as it ships in v3+) the lens garden. A competitor adding a spatial canvas to their chat product won't have any of this; they'll have a dashboard.

The user-facing distinction: in Wovith, the canvas *is* the product. In a competitor's response, the canvas is a *feature* of a chat product. Users tell the difference fast.

### 12.3 What Wovith doesn't do

Wovith does not add chat. Not as the main surface. Not as a sidebar. The premise is that chat-shaped personal AI is the existing market, and that market is well-served by other products. Wovith competes by being *not chat*, not by being a better chat.

The exception: a quick capture cell can accept voice or text input — that's not chat, that's input to a lens. Authoring a new cell via the NL bridge involves writing in natural language — that's not chat either, that's a one-shot translation. There is no ongoing conversation thread anywhere in Wovith. This is a hard line.

## 13. Risks and how they're addressed

The strategic risks that could undermine the GTM.

### 13.1 The category is hard to explain

Spatial-canvas-as-lens-runtime is a phrase that requires unpacking. Audience 3 may bounce before they understand what they're looking at.

**Mitigation:** the homepage hero shows the product visually before any explanation. The first three lines of copy are concrete (lens, cell, canvas). The five-minute onboarding does the heavy lifting of explanation through use. Audience 1 and 2 don't need much explanation; they get it from the tagline.

### 13.2 The agentic budget feels limiting

Users who push the cap on free or even Pro will feel constrained. This is real.

**Mitigation:** the soft cap is clearly communicated. Users see how much they've used and what the cap is. The cap raises with usage maturity (after Day 14, the cap relaxes — users who are still active have shown they're not just exploring). The Trust tier exists for unlimited use.

### 13.3 The mining algorithm proposes a bad first lens

The first lens proposal is the most consequential moment in the product. If it lands wrong, Day-7 retention drops.

**Mitigation:** the mining algorithm is tuned hard against this. Multiple fallbacks (the Captures lens, the Modify flow) catch users who don't love their first proposal. The algorithm gets better with telemetry over the first 3-6 months — early launch is the riskiest moment.

### 13.4 An MCP security incident affects user trust

If a major MCP server (Google Drive's first-party server, for example) has a security breach, Wovith's user trust will suffer regardless of whether Wovith was at fault.

**Mitigation:** the security doc's defense-in-depth posture means Wovith's exposure is bounded. Wovith publicly publishes its incident-response procedure as part of its security documentation. Users see Wovith as the responsible party, even when the underlying issue is upstream.

### 13.5 A competitor commoditizes the lens concept

If Notion or Tana ships "lenses" as a feature, the vocabulary becomes generic.

**Mitigation:** the term "lens" was chosen deliberately because it's already used in adjacent contexts (Apple Vision Pro's lenses, the lenses metaphor in software architecture). It's vocabulary Wovith uses, not vocabulary Wovith owns. If competitors adopt "lens," that's free education for the category — Wovith remains the canonical implementation.

## 15. What this document doesn't yet cover

- **The actual launch date.** The doc treats "v1 launch" as a placeholder. The real date depends on engineering milestones.
- **The legal posture.** Terms of service, privacy policy, EULA — these need separate specification, especially given the OAuth and data residency commitments.
- **The team scaling story.** Wovith launches as a founder-led product. The first 5 hires (likely a designer, a senior backend engineer, a community manager, a content/voice writer, and another product engineer) have not been spec'd here.
- **The international launch.** v1 ships in English, in markets where the OAuth providers (Google) and MCP infrastructure are accessible. Other regions need separate go-to-market work.

These are real and worth addressing in the months following v1, not before.
